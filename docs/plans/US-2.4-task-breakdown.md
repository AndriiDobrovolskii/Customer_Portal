## Notes

- T1/T2 run in parallel — neither depends on the other, per the standard rule.
- T4 bundles two conceptually distinct new-file additions (`app/core/breached_passwords.py` + its data asset) into the service task rather than a separate task, since neither is a distinct execution-skill layer of its own (`core/*` password-handling utilities are added under whichever task first needs them — same precedent as US-2.3 adding `hash_refresh_token` to `core/security.py` under its data-layer task, and adding `send_refresh_reuse_alert` to `core/email.py` under its service task). Here, the breach-check module is called only from `service.py`'s `confirm_password_reset()`, so it belongs with T4 rather than T2.
- `app/core/config.py`'s new settings (reset-token TTL, three rate-limit thresholds/windows, breach-list path) are added under T2, since `cache.py`'s `PasswordResetRateLimitCache` and `repository.py`'s token-TTL logic are the first consumers; T4's breach-list-path setting is available transitively (T4 depends on T3, which depends on T2).
- T7 explicitly includes the cross-module test-fake ripple (`impact-analysis.md`'s flagged risk) as part of its own verification, not a separate task — it's a required side effect of T4's `EmailSender` Protocol change, not a new architectural layer.
- `tests/conftest.py` (plan-review finding, resolved same-day): no dedicated task, by design — `impact-analysis.md` flagged this as uncertain, not required, and no task in T1–T7 needs a new shared fixture (both test files seed their own reset-token/rate-limit state directly, same as US-2.3's own T5+T6 needed no `conftest.py` change). If T6/T7 discover a genuine need while executing, it is added to whichever of those two tasks hits it, not a new task.
